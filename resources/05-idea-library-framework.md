## What the library will contain

Each idea is one row in `idea-library-template.csv`, with these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| `idea_id` | Unique identifier, for example LP-0001 |
| `idea_name` | Short, clear name |
| `category` | One of the categories below |
| `description` | Two sentences: what it is and who it helps |
| `business_model` | One-time sale, subscription, service fee, commission, or other |
| `target_customer` | Specific audience |
| `problem_addressed` | The problem in the customer's words |
| `skills_required` | Skills the owner needs |
| `startup_cost_level` | Low, medium, or high (no invented prices) |
| `tools_needed` | Tools, named generically |
| `competition_research_approach` | How to research alternatives |
| `demand_validation_approach` | How to test demand with real people |
| `first_offer_concept` | A starting offer to test |
| `acquisition_options` | Ways to reach customers |
| `monetisation_method` | How money is earned |
| `main_risks` | Honest risks |
| `first_seven_actions` | Seven concrete steps |
| `detail_level` | `listing` (short) or `guide` (full) |
| `status` | `draft`, `reviewed`, or `verified` |

## Quality rules for every entry

- **Distinct:** The idea must differ from others in customer, problem, or model. Two entries that differ only in a word are duplicates.
- **Deduplicated:** Before adding an entry, search the library for the same name, customer, and model.
- **Honest:** No market-demand numbers, search volumes, profit margins, or success rates unless sourced and dated.
- **Actionable:** The seven first actions must be steps a beginner can complete.
- **Risk-stated:** Every entry names at least one real risk.

## Status levels

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `draft` | Written, not yet checked |
| `reviewed` | Checked for duplicates and clarity |
| `verified` | Checked for accuracy of tools, platform rules, and costs, and reviewed by a second person |

Only `verified` entries count toward the 1,000+ claim.

## Niche selection scorecard (decision aid)

Score each idea from 1 to 5 on each criterion. Scores help you compare options. They are not proof that an idea will succeed.

| Criterion | What to ask |
|-----------|-------------|
| Skills | Do I already have the skills, or can I learn them quickly? |
| Interests | Will I still want to work on this in month three? |
| Available time | Can I deliver it in my weekly hours? |
| Available budget | Can I start without spending money I cannot afford to lose? |
| Customer accessibility | Can I reach these customers? |
| Problem importance | Do real people describe this as a painful problem? (Check through interviews.) |
| Ability to test demand | Can I test interest in under two weeks? |
| Competition | Are there alternatives, and can I offer something different? |
| Delivery complexity | Can I deliver consistently with my current skills? |
| Business model fit | Does the model suit my preferences and resources? |

Total out of 50. Use the total to choose what to test next, not what to trust blindly.

## Demand validation methods

- **Customer interviews:** Ask about the problem and what people already do, not whether they like your idea.
- **Competitor research:** Look at what alternatives charge and what customers complain about in public reviews.
- **Search behaviour:** Use the platform's own search or keyword tools to see what people ask. Record the date and source.
- **Small experiments:** A waitlist, a simple sample, or a pre-sale with a clear refund policy.
- **Pre-launch validation:** Test your landing page with real visitors before you build the full product.
