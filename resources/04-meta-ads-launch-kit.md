# LaunchPilot Meta Ads Launch Kit

An educational guide to preparing accounts, planning campaigns, testing ads, measuring results, and staying within Meta's advertising rules.

**Verify before you act.** Meta changes its tools, menu names, and policies often. Before you set up anything, check the current Meta Business Help Centre and Meta Advertising Standards. This kit does not promise results, benchmarks, or approval of any ad.

This kit is not affiliated with or endorsed by Meta. Use Meta's brand marks only as Meta's brand guidelines allow.

## Part A: Business preparation

Do not spend on ads until these are true.

- [ ] **Offer clarity:** You can describe the product, price, billing type, and deliverables in one short paragraph.
- [ ] **Landing page ready:** The page is live, loads on a phone, and every claim matches a real deliverable.
- [ ] **Payment and delivery tested:** You have completed a test purchase (or sign-up) and received the product yourself.
- [ ] **Customer support ready:** A real email or contact method is listed, and you know who answers it and how quickly.
- [ ] **Refund and privacy information:** Refund policy, privacy notice, and terms are visible and linked from the footer.

## Part C: Campaign planning

**Choose a campaign objective** that matches your goal (names may change; check current Ads Manager options):

- **Awareness:** People see your brand. Useful when a product is new and you need reach.
- **Traffic:** People visit your landing page. Useful for testing interest.
- **Leads:** People submit a sign-up form. Useful for waitlists or free samples.
- **Sales:** People buy. Only use when checkout and tracking work.

**Audience hypotheses** (write one per campaign; test them, do not assume them):

| Hypothesis | Who | Why you think they care | How you will test it |

Use only audience options Meta allows for your category. Do not target or describe people by sensitive personal attributes (for example, health conditions, religion, or financial hardship). Do not use targeting that implies personal characteristics.

**Creative concepts:** Plan at least three different angles before you build anything (see Part D).

**Copy variations:** For each creative, write at least two headline and body options. Keep the claim the same across variations so you are testing presentation, not promises.

**Budget limits:** Start with a small daily budget you can afford to lose entirely. Set a total maximum you will not exceed during the test.

**Conversion tracking:** Before launch, confirm that the Meta Pixel (or your checkout's approved tracking) fires on the landing page and on the purchase or sign-up confirmation. Check Events Manager to see whether events arrive. Do not send personal customer data to Meta.

## Part D: Creative testing

Test one type of change at a time.

| Message type | What it shows | Example angle (no claims) |
|--------------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Problem-aware | The problem the customer already feels | "Not sure which online business to start?" |
| Benefit-led | What the customer can do with the product | "Get a step-by-step plan for your first offer." |
| Product demonstration | A real screen or page from the product | Short screen recording of a template being used |
| Educational | Useful tips that lead to the product | "3 questions to ask before choosing a niche." |
| Different hook | The first 3 seconds of a video or the first line of an image | Compare a question, a statement, and a number from your own material |

**CTA rules:** Use accurate calls to action such as "Learn more", "Get the guide", or "Sign up". Do not use "Guaranteed", "Get rich", "Easy money", or any before-and-after claims.

**Creative rules:** Use your own product, your own screens, and real people who agreed to appear. Do not use stock testimonials or fake reviews.

## Part E: Measurement in plain language

| Term | Plain meaning | How to read it |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| CPM | Cost per 1,000 times your ad was shown | Tells you the price of reach. Compare within your own tests, not against other businesses. |
| CTR | Share of people who saw the ad and clicked | Shows whether the message gets attention. |
| CPC | Average cost of each click | Shows how expensive it is to get a visitor. |
| Landing-page views | Visitors whose page actually loaded | Clicks that never load are a technical problem. |
| Conversion rate | Share of visitors who buy or sign up | Shows how well the page works. |
| Cost per purchase | Total ad spend divided by purchases | The most important number for a sales campaign. |
| Refund rate | Share of purchases refunded | A high rate signals a mismatch between the ad and the product. |
| Contribution margin | What you keep from each sale after direct costs (product, payment fees, delivery, ad cost) | Tells you whether a sale covers its own costs. |

**Do not invent benchmarks.** Compare each number with your own earlier tests. Record your starting numbers first.

## Part F: Optimisation

1. **Find the weak point.** Low CTR points to the creative or audience. High CPC with good CTR may point to delivery or competition. Strong traffic with low sales points to the landing page or offer.
2. **Separate traffic from page problems.** If visitors leave before the page finishes loading, fix speed before changing the ad.
3. **Change one meaningful thing at a time.** For example, change only the headline, or only the audience.
4. **Let the test run long enough.** Wait for a reasonable number of clicks and purchases before judging. Do not decide on a handful of results.
5. **Track total campaign cost**, including ad spend, tools, payment fees, and refunds.
6. **Do not scale a campaign that loses money** on its total costs. Pause it, learn from it, and test again.

## Part G: Compliance checklist

- [ ] I reviewed the current Meta advertising policies and special-category rules for my product.
- [ ] No income, earnings, or guaranteed-results claims.
- [ ] No misleading before-and-after claims.
- [ ] No targeting based on sensitive personal attributes, and no ad copy that implies a personal attribute about the viewer.
- [ ] Creative shows the real product and real people who agreed to appear.
- [ ] Landing page matches the ad. Nothing is promised in the ad that the page does not deliver.
- [ ] Refund, privacy, and contact information are visible.
- [ ] I used Meta's brand marks only as their brand guidelines allow, and I do not imply any partnership with Meta.
